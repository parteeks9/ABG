Strategy 1: Going Horizontal

Start with 5 group companies in 6–9 months. Once we see results, we replicate the same agents across the rest of the group — at a fraction of the original cost.

✓ Advantages

  • 5 group companies go live in 6–9 months — success drives rollout to the rest
  • Builds supporters across HR, Finance, and Legal teams
  • Once built for 5 companies, rolling out to others costs 70% less per company
  • Proven results at 5 companies makes the group-wide case automatically

✗ Limitations

  • Shallow impact — no transformational use case
  • Won't deliver the most complex industrial AI use cases
  • Risk of being seen as a routine IT project
  • HR/Finance/Legal heads are not P&L owners

Recommendation: 3-Phase Implementation Plan

A structured rollout that builds confidence early, proves ROI by Month 12, and goes deep into one group company from Year 2 onwards.

Phase 1
Build Confidence
Month 1–6

Select AI use cases where the conditions are already in your favour — these are the ones most likely to succeed fast and build internal trust.

1

Data is in good shape

The data needed for the AI model already exists, is digital, and is reasonably clean. No new data collection required before you can start building.

2

Proven technology

Other companies globally have already solved this problem with AI. You are not doing research — you are applying a known approach to your context.

3

Limited regulatory involvement

No RBI, IRDAI, or SEBI approvals needed before going live. The AI operates in an advisory or read-only capacity — humans remain in control.

4

Easy management buy-in

The business leader who owns this process is already open to AI. No long approval chain — you can start a pilot without a board decision.

5

Low integration complexity

The AI can plug into existing systems without a major IT project. No deep SCADA connections, no core banking rewrites — just APIs and existing data feeds.

Phase 2
Scale What Works
Month 7–12

With Phase 1 agents live and credibility established, shift focus to use cases that deliver clear financial returns and can be replicated across multiple group companies.

1

Prioritise measurable ROI

Choose use cases where the financial impact can be tracked and reported within the quarter. The board needs to see numbers, not just activity.

2

Build for replicability

Every agent built in this phase should be designed so it can be deployed at another group company with minimal rework. One build, multiple beneficiaries.

3

Extend Phase 1 wins

Take the agents that worked well in Phase 1 and roll them out to the remaining pilot companies. Let proven results sell the programme internally.

4

Increase automation confidence

Agents that ran in advisory mode in Phase 1 can now be given more autonomy where the accuracy data supports it. Move from human-in-loop to human-on-loop.

Phase 3
Go Vertical
Year 2 onwards

Pick one group company and go deep. Embed a dedicated AI team, solve its most complex operational problems, and build a sector playbook that can be exported to sister companies.

1

Choose the right anchor company

Use the company selection score from Strategy 2 to identify the best fit. Strong leadership buy-in, reasonable data readiness, and high replicability to other group companies are the key criteria.

2

Solve core operational problems

Go beyond support functions. Target the AI use cases that are central to how that business makes money — production efficiency, demand planning, customer retention, or risk management.

3

Build a sector playbook

Document every decision, integration, and lesson learned. The goal is a repeatable blueprint that makes deploying the same AI at the next group company significantly faster and cheaper.

4

Let results drive group-wide adoption

A business leader presenting real outcomes at the Group Leadership Council is more persuasive than any strategy document. One deep success unlocks the rest of the group.

Birla AI Labs — Technology Options

AI agent deployment strategy

Two options evaluated — Fast Lane vs Build Lane — with a recommended path forward

⚡

Fast lane

Deploy agents on top of enterprise platforms ABG already licenses — Microsoft 365, SAP Build, AWS Bedrock

8–12 wks
First agent live
₹0
New licences needed

Benefits

✓

Fastest path to board visibility

Working agents in 90 days — without waiting for a platform build. Critical for a company still hiring its team.

✓

Uses licences ABG already pays for

Microsoft 365 (Copilot Studio), SAP BTP (Build Process Automation), AWS Bedrock. Zero incremental vendor cost in Year 1.

✓

Lower talent dependency early

Agents can be built by product managers and business analysts while specialist ML engineers are still being hired.

✓

Enterprise-grade reliability from day one

Microsoft and SAP SLAs, security certifications, and uptime commitments included. BAL team carries no maintenance burden.

Key considerations

⚠

Use case ceiling. No vendor platform supports industrial AI — kiln control, smelter optimisation, or credit scoring ML cannot be built here.

⚠

Vendor dependency. Platform pricing changes and API deprecations can break agents without notice. Once embedded, negotiating leverage reduces.

⚠

Data residency must be verified. M365 and SAP tenants must be confirmed in the India region before any sensitive HR or financial data is processed.

⚠

Limited IP ownership. Agents built inside Microsoft or SAP platforms carry their IP terms — not pure ABG assets that can be monetised externally.

Best suited for

HR self-service AP automation NDA processing Document intelligence GST / TDS compliance
🏗

Build lane

Assemble a proprietary agent platform using open-source components — hosted entirely within ABG's cloud environment

Month 6–7
Platform ready
60%
Lower cost vs SaaS at scale

Benefits

✓

Full data sovereignty

ABG's operational, financial, and subscriber data never leaves group infrastructure. Critical for RBI, IRDAI, and DPDP Act 2023 compliance across regulated entities.

✓

No ceiling on complexity

Supports RL kiln control, smelter AI, custom credit models, and any use case requiring purpose-built ML — none deliverable on any vendor platform.

✓

100% ABG IP ownership

Every model, agent, and dataset is a proprietary ABG asset. Positions BAL for potential external monetisation and licencing in Year 4–5.

✓

Superior long-term economics

₹6–8 Cr/yr at group scale vs ₹15–25 Cr/yr for equivalent SaaS. Break-even at 18–24 months, significant savings beyond that point.

Key considerations

⚠

Longer setup time. Platform assembly takes 10–12 weeks with a dedicated team. No agents deploy on this stack before Month 6–7, requiring board patience.

⚠

Specialist talent required. Needs ML engineers, MLOps specialists, and platform engineers — among the hardest profiles to hire and retain in India today.

⚠

BAL owns the maintenance. Uptime, security patches, and platform upgrades are BAL's responsibility. Requires a dedicated platform engineering function from Year 1.

⚠

Higher upfront investment. ₹8–12 Cr platform build before the first vertical agent deploys. Board must accept an investment phase before seeing complex AI outcomes.

Best suited for

Kiln & smelter AI Credit underwriting Fraud detection Churn prediction Demand forecasting
🏆

Recommendation: run both in parallel — dual-track strategy

Neither option alone is sufficient. Fast Lane gives the board visible results in 90 days. Build Lane delivers the transformational AI that justifies the programme's existence. The two tracks are not alternatives — they serve different use cases and must run simultaneously from Month 1.

⚡ Fast lane — Month 1 to 9

Deploy 8 horizontal agents (HR, Finance, Legal) on M365 + SAP platforms already licensed. First agent live in 8–12 weeks. Board sees measurable P&L impact at the Month 9 gate. Zero new vendor cost.

🏗 Build lane — Month 3 to 36

3-person platform team assembles open-source stack (LangGraph + Claude API + Pinecone) by Month 6–7. All vertical agents — kiln AI, smelter AI, credit AI, churn AI — built on this from Month 8 onwards.
